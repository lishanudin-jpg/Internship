01

A Life Built on Dreams

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist, teacher and statesman who became the 11th President of India. His journey from Rameswaram to the highest constitutional office of the country became a powerful example of how education, discipline and determination can transform a life.

ABOUT HIM

Scientist. Teacher. Leader.

01

Humble Beginnings

Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a modest family, he developed a strong interest in learning and science during his school years. His early life taught him the value of hard work, simplicity and perseverance.

02

A Scientist with a Vision

After studying aerospace engineering, Kalam worked with India's space and defence programmes. He became closely associated with the development of launch vehicle and missile technologies and earned the popular title "Missile Man of India."

03

The People's President

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. During his presidency, he connected strongly with students and young people. He used public platforms to encourage curiosity, education, innovation and responsible citizenship.

04

A Teacher at Heart

After leaving the presidency, Kalam continued to teach, write and interact with students. His books, speeches and conversations consistently encouraged young people to think beyond limitations and contribute to India's future.

HIS JOURNEY

Milestones That Shaped a Nation

1931

Born in Rameswaram

Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

1954

Graduated in Physics

He completed his physics degree at Saint Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli.

1958

Entered Aerospace Research

Kalam joined India's defence research establishment and began his professional journey as an aerospace scientist.

1980

SLV-III Achievement

Under his leadership, the SLV-III programme successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit.

1997

Bharat Ratna

Kalam received India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, for his contributions to scientific research and national development.

2002

Became President of India

He was elected as the 11th President of India and served from 2002 to 2007.

2015

A Legacy Continues

Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture to students at IIM Shillong. His commitment to education continues to inspire millions.

HIS LEGACY

More Than a Scientist

Kalam's greatest legacy is not limited to rockets, satellites or missiles. It lies in the confidence he placed in young people and their ability to shape the future.

He believed that education should create curiosity, courage and a willingness to solve real problems. His simple lifestyle and approachable personality made him a role model far beyond the scientific community.

Even today, his life encourages students to combine knowledge with character, ambition with responsibility, and dreams with meaningful action.
